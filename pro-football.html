Communicating intent through controls.

The control design explored how a simple mobile input can express more than one action through direction, hold duration, swipe/flick gestures and context-sensitive assistance.

Move & sprint

Direction + magnitude

Ground / lofted pass

Through ball

Low and chipped variants target space ahead of a teammate and can cue an AI run into the intended channel.

Space + run intent

Shot variants

Regular, chipped, controlled and stronger shot concepts vary trajectory and intent through input gesture and power.

Power + direction

Shield & protect

Hold possession under pressure, creating a decision window for a pass, a turn or a carry when space opens.

Pressure context

Press & call pressure

Defensive input closes space; a separate call can cue the nearest teammate to pressure the opponent.

Direct + AI assist

Switch player

Defensive selection considers proximity to the ball, opponent direction and velocity, and interception opportunities.

Selection assistance

Tackle & matchup

Tackling is velocity/direction sensitive; matchup sets a controlled defensive stance and helps contest dribbling and lanes.

Timing + positioning
Design consideration: assisted targeting can lower input friction, but the player still needs to understand which teammate or space their directional intent is selecting. Input queues and overwrite rules should remain predictable.

08 / RATIONALE
Connected design

Decisions are about the relationship between systems.

The most important decisions sit at the intersections between controls, tactical behaviour, animation, audio and player learning.

Control

Assistance vs. agency

Assisted targeting can help with fast touch input, but must still respect the direction the player indicates and keep the intended outcome readable.

Tactical AI

Role specificity vs. team shape

Role tendencies give teammates personality, while possession, ball location, pressure and nearby players determine which behaviour is useful next.

Audio

Variety vs. relevance

Event and zone context can make commentary and crowd response feel situational; priority and variation rules help avoid repetitive or overlapping cues.

Animation

Motion quality vs. responsiveness

A convincing motion should still connect cleanly to the input, ball contact and next action. Review needs gameplay context as well as clip quality.

Control clarity

Directional intent and assisted targeting remain legible at match speed, especially when passing, receiving and shooting under pressure.

Team intelligence

Role tendencies work together with the formation: off-ball support, defensive cover and pressing response should serve the same team shape.

Consistent feedback

Animation, player sounds, commentary, crowd response and UI cues reinforce the same match event without competing for attention.

Queueing / overwrite rule

Passing and shooting inputs can be queued while receiving. A later input overwrites the earlier queued action, so the visible action cue should keep the next intent predictable.

Action definition combines input intent, contextual conditions, response and feedback so every control has a clear role in the match loop.

03Role-based AI & state transitionsAttackers, midfielders, defenders and goalkeeper logic＋

My contribution I worked on AI behaviours for formations and football roles. The role states and transitions connect possession, pressure, pitch position and teammate/opponent actions.

Attacking roles

StateIntentExample transition condition
PositioningMaintain a useful attacking position.Teammate gains midfield possession → make a run; ball played into box → track rebounds.
Making a runMove into space to receive a pass.Through ball played → receive/dribble; run blocked → return to positioning.
Pressing defenderApply pressure after possession loss.Ball won → possession action; defender clears → return to positioning.
Tracking reboundsMove toward second balls and deflections.Ball deflected toward goal → shot opportunity; opponent clears → press/reposition.
DribblingCarry the ball forward.Pressure closes → shield; better teammate option → pass; clear shot → shoot.
Passing / crossingDeliver to a teammate or into the box.Pass succeeds → support/run; pass fails → defensive response; no cross target → pass/carry.
ShootingAttempt goal.Shot blocked → track rebound; possession resolves → reposition.

Role tendencies

  • ST: prioritises scoring positions in the box and reacts quickly to rebounds.
  • SS: connects midfield and the forward line, combining with the CF/ST rather than always shooting.
  • CF: offers hold-up and link-up play, can drop deeper to receive and bring teammates into play.
  • LW: tends to preserve width and cross; may cut inside when the wide route is blocked.
  • RW: more likely to cut inside and support the striker, with crossing as an alternative.

Midfield roles

StateIntentExample transition condition
Holding positionMaintain midfield zone relative to match flow.Ball enters defensive third → track back; possession regained → position for attack.
Tracking backSupport the defence.Opponent enters penalty area → press; interception → counter-attack positioning.
Blocking passing lanesCut off likely passing routes.Pass intercepted → counter-attack; opponent switches zone → reposition.
Pressing opponentApply pressure to the ball carrier.Opponent loses ball → counter-attack positioning; press fails → hold/reposition.
Positioning for attackOffer a progressive passing option.Forward run → through ball; no option → dribble / short pass.
Short / long / through passRetain possession, switch play or play into space.Successful pass → support; intercepted pass → defensive reaction.
  • DMF: prioritises defensive cover and passing-lane protection.
  • CMF: balances attack and defence with varied passing options.
  • AMF: looks to create chances, play through balls and occupy advanced areas.
  • LMF/RMF: preserve width, cross from wide areas and can support fullbacks.

Defender roles

Out of possession: hold the defensive line, mark opponents, shift the line laterally, track runners, press, recover defensive shape and block passing lanes. In possession: position for build-up, shield, play short/long passes, clear danger and track rebounds. CB/LB/RB responsibilities follow their defensive line role and the ball-side context.

Goalkeeper states

Positioning in the box → preparing for a shot → saving attempt. A ball played over the defence triggers a sweeper-keeper decision; within reach can lead to clearing, while an opponent arriving first can create a one-on-one situation. When the team gains possession, the keeper can prepare distribution.

Goalkeeper behaviour connects positioning, shot preparation, intervention, one-on-one decisions and distribution into a clear defensive-to-possession loop.

04Formation behaviour: six tactical shapesBalanced, attacking and defensive role arrangements＋

My contribution Formation gameplay, including 4–3–3 and 3–4–3, formed part of my AI behaviour work. The formation library expands that role-based thinking into six clearly differentiated tactical references.

IntentFormationRole arrangementAI priorities
Balanced4–3–3Back four, holding midfielder with two central midfielders, wide forwards and a central striker.Connect build-up to the front three, preserve midfield passing options and coordinate full-back support.
Balanced4–2–3–1Back four, double pivot, three attacking midfielders and one striker.Keep central cover behind the creator, offer multiple receiving lanes and support the lone forward.
Attacking3–4–3Three centre-backs, two wide midfield/wing-back roles, two central midfielders and a front three.Exploit width, maintain forward pressure and coordinate wide recovery with central protection.
Attacking4–2–4Back four, two central midfielders, two wide forwards and two central forwards.Keep multiple targets ahead of the ball, create quick combinations in the final third and protect transition space around the midfield pair.
Defensive4–5–1Back four, a five-player midfield line and one central forward outlet.Close central passing lanes, shift as a compact midfield unit and retain an outlet for counter-attacks.
Defensive5–4–1Back five, four midfielders in a narrow/compact line and one forward outlet.Protect the box, track wide overloads with coordinated shifts and transition quickly toward the forward outlet after regaining possession.

Formation-aware role behaviour

  • Attackers: ST prioritises scoring positions and rebound reactions; CF connects play and can shield; SS links midfield with the front line; LW and RW balance width, crossing and inside runs.
  • Midfielders: DMF protects central lanes and supports the defensive structure; CMF connects phases; AMF finds advanced pockets and creative passing options; LMF/RMF provide width and recovery support.
  • Defenders: CB protects the central channel and tracks runners; LB/RB balance wide coverage with support in possession; wing-back roles in three-defender shapes carry increased width and recovery responsibility.
  • Goalkeeper: adjusts positioning as the ball and defensive line move, prepares for shots, decides when to sweep, and transitions into distribution after possession is secured.

The six shapes form a shared tactical reference library. My formation-AI work focused on 4–3–3 and 3–4–3; the full library compares all six shapes using the same role vocabulary. For additional tactical context, see the football formations guide and formation principles.

06Movement, ball handling & assistanceTrajectory, player stability, target assistance and queued actions＋

System specification The input material describes how trajectory, input power, player direction and movement state influence ball actions.

  • Ground passes can curve and may bounce at long distance/high power; lofted passes use an arc to reach the receiver.
  • Through balls are intended for the forward path of a receiver and gaps between defenders; a chipped through ball can be used over a line.
  • Shot trajectory is informed by power and distance; shot direction is associated with goal direction and directional input.
  • Passing/shooting accuracy can be reduced when the player runs and acts beyond their facing/vision, with player stability also affected around the action.
  • The latest pre-input can replace an earlier queued action while the player is receiving the ball.
  • Aerial ball reception can automatically trigger head/chest actions according to the action context.
  • Set-piece aiming uses directional input and a line indicator; line appearance can communicate selected power.

The movement model connects action power, trajectory, player facing and stability. These relationships give the team clear tuning dimensions for pass and shot behaviour.

07Motion-matching animation reviewCoverage, animation analysis, coordination and validation＋

My contribution I provided animation analysis and coordinated the motion-matching workstream in an animation-supervision role, connecting motion quality to gameplay intent and ball interaction.

Coverage categories from the source inventory

  • Ball control: idle, acceleration, close/far ball distance, turns and directional variations.
  • Locomotion: walking, running, sprinting, acceleration/deceleration and directional changes.
  • Ball actions: ground/lofted passing, shots, dribbling actions, aerial receptions and transitions.
  • Defensive actions: pressure, tackle/slide, deflection, trips/falls and recovery.
  • Match response: reactions and celebrations.

Review sequence

  1. Map which action/direction/speed contexts have animation coverage.
  2. Inspect motion relative to intended player input, ball position and direction.
  3. Review contact timing, posture, head/shoulder intent, clipping/drift and turn continuity.
  4. Coordinate actionable feedback with animation and implementation stakeholders.
  5. Revisit the action in gameplay context to check response and transition quality.

The review process translates individual observations into actionable feedback on coverage, contact, body orientation, turn continuity and gameplay responsiveness.

10FTUE / tutorial specificationObjective, demonstration, input, success and retry＋

System specification Tutorials use a guided sequence: objective prompt, visual demonstration, highlighted input, player action, success/failure feedback and progression.

Tutorial flow template

BeatRequirementSuccess / failure handling
PromptState the immediate objective in one short instruction.Keep the goal visible while the player prepares.
DemonstrateShow a thumb/gesture cue and highlight the relevant control.Do not obscure the play space or required control.
ActAsk for one specific action: move + shoot; pass/switch/shoot; tackle; intercept.Evaluate the stated objective condition.
ResolveGive visible/audible success feedback.Success advances to next step.
RetryReset the local exercise if the objective fails.Offer a clear retry and a dismiss/skip affordance where intended.

Examples

  • Move and shoot: move into the highlighted region; shoot when prompted; proceed on success or repeat the segment.
  • Pass, switch and shoot: direct the pass toward a highlighted teammate; pass; switch/control the receiver; shoot near goal.
  • Tackle and recover: move toward the ball carrier; press tackle at the relevant moment; verify possession was regained within the highlighted scenario.
  • Intercept and transition: perform the directed interception/pass sequence before progressing to the next action.

Success advances the learning sequence; failure returns the player to a focused retry with the objective and relevant control cue still clear.

13Design quality principlesRules for clarity, consistency and tactical readability＋

The quality bar for a football system is consistency between what the player intends, what the match resolves, and how the team communicates the result. These principles connect the control, AI, animation, audio and FTUE work described throughout this case study.

PrincipleDesign ruleWhat it supports
Readable intentInput direction, selected target and action feedback should agree.Trust in passing, shooting and player switching.
Context-aware AIRole tendencies respond to possession, ball-side, pressure, available lanes and teammate movement.Off-ball behaviours that feel connected to the team's shape.
Formation coherenceWide roles, central cover and forward support should reinforce the selected tactical intent.Clear differences between balanced, attacking and defensive shapes.
Animation continuityPose, turn, foot placement and ball contact should support the intended action and its next state.Responsive control and readable physical interaction.
Audio hierarchyHigh-value events take priority while commentary and crowd layers remain context-sensitive.Feedback that adds meaning without masking active play.
Learn-by-doingTeach one action at a time with a visible objective, focused input cue and clear outcome.FTUE that moves from instruction into player agency.

Balancing checklist

  • Check pass and shot direction against the player's indicated intent.
  • Review role spacing while the ball shifts across thirds and possession changes.
  • Compare each formation's support lanes, defensive cover and forward options.
  • Keep tutorial prompts and controls readable in the match composition.
  • Prioritise whistle, goal and major match-state events over lower-priority sound layers.
  • Review motion-matching transitions in gameplay context, especially turns, contact and ball-control actions.
